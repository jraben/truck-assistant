Three ways to put a screen in the Slate

Three plans were written separately, against the same ten wants and the same Slate accessories, then put side by side here. Prices are parts only, before your Slate accessories.

Recommendation

Android head unit (TEYES CC3 360) + Carlinkit

It covers every want, nine of them natively: CarPlay with Waze, a real browser, four cameras with 360° stitching, a built-in amp, and ignition and reverse wires. It has no battery to swell in a Phoenix cab. It costs about the same as the Pi 5 build and needs the least custom hardware, which also makes it the easiest for other Slate owners to copy.

The tradeoff is the look. CarPlay runs in its own app beside our dashboard, not inside it. If having CarPlay framed by our own gauges matters more than fewer weekends of wiring, pick the Pi 5. Pi + ESP32 is the cheapest by about $350, but Waze and the browser leave the screen.

Two corrections to the first prototype, from the ESP32 plan's research: the Pi 5 has no hardware H.264 decoder (the Pi 4 does), and the ESP32-P4 encodes H.264 in hardware but decodes it only in software.

Unknowns all three share

  1. Truck data. Slate has published no OBD or CAN access, so the truck's charge level and energy per mile start out typed in or estimated.
  2. Speakers. Slate has no factory audio, and how its integrated speakers take sound (wired or Bluetooth) is unpublished. That's a ±$150 swing, settled at delivery.
  3. Tablet Mount size. Don't buy a screen bigger than 10" until the clamp is measured.
  4. Five Switch. A 5-gang plate for the Accessory Console. We can read it and add relays in parallel; controlling the factory circuits waits for the wiring.
  5. Phoenix heat. Screens black out and parts warp. Print in ASA, shade the screen, and log one summer before publishing.

What to build now, whichever wins

  1. The dashboard web app. It runs on all three screens.
  2. The ESP32 truck box: relays, Five Switch sense, tilt, cabin temp and TPMS over BLE, MQTT to HA.
  3. Slate Hub portal: settings, accounts, trip history on the NAS.
  4. The charging finder with your preferences (AFDC + Open Charge Map).
  5. Printed parts that don't depend on the screen: camera brackets, hitch-cam mount, cable clips.

The screen choice only decides the last mile, so it can wait until the truck and its mount arrive.
